Mission & scope

The Review exists to publish research that is rigorous without being inaccessible, and accessible without sacrificing rigor. We cover economics, philosophy, politics, public policy, education, and technology & society — six fields we treat as facets of a single, older question: how people organize, govern, argue, and build shared institutions.

Our editorial team and our contributor base are international by design. A paper is accepted on the strength of its argument and evidence, not the geography of its author.

"Civic" describes not a nationality but a method — the disciplined study of how shared life is organized.

Editorial independence

The Civic Review is not affiliated with any government, political party, university, or corporate sponsor. Editorial decisions — what is commissioned, what is accepted, what is corrected — rest solely with the editorial board, governed by the standards set out in our editing guidelines.

Publication practice

Issues are released on a bi-weekly cadence. Each volume carries a short editorial introduction situating that issue's papers within a broader theme, though papers are also indexed individually by field in the archive.

The Review accepts original research across all six of its fields on a rolling basis. Submissions are read by the editorial team in the order received; most authors hear back within three weeks.

  • Manuscripts should generally run 1,000–6,000 words

  • Include full citations for all sources

— Editing Guidelines

How the Review edits and evaluates research

These guidelines describe the standards, structure, and process the Review applies to every submission — for authors, editors, and readers alike.

01 — Purpose & philosophy 02 — Research standards 03 — Writing style 04 — Paper structure 05 — Citations & references 06 — Fact-checking & sources 07 — Editorial review process 08 — Language & tone 09 — Academic integrity 10 — AI & research 11 — Corrections

01Purpose & editorial philosophy

The Review publishes rigorous, accessible, interdisciplinary research across the humanities, economics, philosophy, public policy, and business. We value intellectual independence, clarity of argument, evidence-based reasoning, and dialogue across disciplinary lines over adherence to any single school of thought.

02Research standards

Accepted work states a clear research question, offers original analysis rather than summary, draws on appropriate evidence, cites sources accurately, is transparent about methodology, argues logically from premise to conclusion, upholds academic integrity throughout, and engages seriously with existing scholarship in its field.

03Writing style

Writing should be precise, concise, and analytical — accessible to an interdisciplinary readership rather than a narrow specialist audience. Avoid unnecessary jargon; where a specialist concept is essential, explain it rather than assume it. Every paper should be structured around one clear argument.

04Structure of research papers

The recommended structure — flexible by discipline — is: title; author and affiliation; abstract; keywords; introduction; research question or thesis; methodology or approach; analysis; discussion; conclusion; and references.

05Citations & references

Authors should use a single citation system consistently throughout a paper. Guidance is available for citing books, journal articles, websites, government publications, datasets, interviews, and primary sources — consult the editorial team if your source type isn't covered.

06Fact-checking & sources

The Review favors peer-reviewed research, academic books, government publications, official datasets, primary documents, and reputable institutional sources. Unsupported claims and citation padding — sources listed but not meaningfully engaged with — are both discouraged and checked for during review.

07Editorial review process

Every submission moves through the same sequence before publication:

03Research & fact check

06Final approval
07Publication

08Language & tone

Intellectual, measured, analytical, evidence-based, and accessible. The Review does not publish sensationalism or clickbait — nor does it reward overwrought academic prose written to impress rather than inform.

09Originality & academic integrity

Authors are responsible for the originality of their work and for attributing all sources used. Plagiarism, data fabrication or manipulation, and undisclosed conflicts of interest are grounds for rejection or, if discovered post-publication, retraction.

10AI & research

Responsible use of AI tools is permitted for brainstorming, language editing, formatting, and preliminary research assistance. Authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, citations, arguments, data, and final quality of the submitted work. AI-generated citations or claims must never be presented as verified research without independent confirmation.

11Editorial corrections

Minor errors are corrected with a visible editorial notice on the published paper. Substantive errors may prompt an author correction or, where the paper's core claims are compromised, a retraction. A correction record is maintained and displayed alongside every published paper it applies to.
